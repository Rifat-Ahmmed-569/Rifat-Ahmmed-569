I build robots and the embedded systems that bring them to life: firmware, sensors, motor control, and the interfaces that keep hardware and software working together.

I'm interested in the layer where things actually fail. A sensor gives inconsistent readings. A motor driver behaves differently under load. A communication link drops when the system moves into the field.

That's where engineering becomes interesting.

**How I work**

- **Understand the layer beneath.** If I don't understand why something works, I don't fully trust that it works.
- **Debugging is part of engineering.** Noisy grounds, timing issues in interrupts, unreliable sensors, and unexpected behavior under real conditions are not distractions. They are the work.
- **Reliability over demonstrations.** Working once proves a concept. Working consistently under real conditions proves engineering.
- **Build with people.** Difficult problems become manageable when a team can share ideas, challenge assumptions, and openly say, "I broke it." I try to build that kind of environment.
